# Week 3: Evaluating AI Output

**Surfaces:** S1 [E] · **Capabilities:** C5 Model selection and effort levels [S] · **4D:** Discernment

## Goals
Students will be able to:
- Explain why models hallucinate and name the situations where errors are most likely.
- Apply verification habits: check claims against sources, recompute numbers, test code, ask for uncertainty.
- Recognize confident errors and sycophancy (the model agreeing with you because you pushed).
- Use the Description–Discernment loop to improve results instead of accepting the first answer.

## Before class
- Claude Academy: **AI Fluency: Framework and foundations**, lessons 10–11 (Discernment; the Description–Discernment loop).
- Claude Academy: **AI capabilities and limitations** (begin; finish by week 5).

## Session 1 (75 min)
- 25 min: Why outputs are wrong. Hallucination, outdated knowledge, arithmetic, reasoning slips, misread instructions, sycophancy. Live examples.
- 25 min: Verification habits. Source tracing, recomputation, cross-checking with a second model, asking the model to critique itself (and the limits of that).
- 25 min: Practice. Students get five AI answers, some subtly wrong, and find the problems.

## Session 2 (75 min)
- 15 min: Does a bigger model or more thinking help? Effort levels and reasoning models (C5) on problems where the fast model fails.
- 50 min: Lab. Students try to make an AI fail in their own field, then document how they detected it.
- 10 min: Share-out. Build a class "failure gallery".

## Assignment: Fact-check and failure report
Part A: take a 600–800 word AI-generated explainer on a topic you know well, verify every factual claim, and annotate each as correct, wrong, or unverifiable, with a source. Part B: find and document two failures of your own (prompt, output, how you caught it, how you fixed it).
- **500 extension:** test whether a higher effort setting or a different model fixes each failure, and report the result.
- Estimated time: 3 hours.

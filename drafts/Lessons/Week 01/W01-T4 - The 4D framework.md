# Week 1: The 4D framework

**COSC-300/500 Applied AI Essentials** · 4D focus: Delegation

## What you will be able to do

- Name the four skills of AI fluency and say what each one covers.
- Explain why delegation is the first decision you make when you use AI.
- Use four questions to decide whether a task should go to AI.
- Describe the ways a task can be shared between you and AI.

## Four skills

Knowing which buttons to press in an AI tool is the easy part, and it goes out of date quickly. What lasts is judgment about how to work with these tools. Throughout the course we use the four skills of AI fluency from Anthropic's *AI Fluency* course:

| Skill | The question it answers | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Delegation** | Should AI do this, and how much of it? | Deciding to draft your own cover letter and ask for feedback, instead of asking for the letter |
| **Description** | How do I communicate what I want? | Saying who the reader is, how long it should be, and what to leave out |
| **Discernment** | Is what came back any good? | Opening the cited source to see whether it says what the answer claims |
| **Diligence** | Am I taking responsibility for the result? | Disclosing that AI helped, and standing behind every claim you submit |

The four work as a sequence. You decide what to hand over, describe it, judge the result, and answer for what you do with it. They also loop. What you find when you judge an answer usually sends you back to describe the task better.

Each week of the course emphasizes one of the four, shown at the top of every lesson. Over fifteen weeks each skill returns several times, applied to more capable tools. Description starts as a prompt in Week 2 and becomes a task brief for an agent in Week 6 and a specification for software in Week 10.

## This week: Delegation

This week is **Delegation**, because it comes first. Before you open a chat, decide whether this is a job for AI.

People usually skip this step. The tool is open, the task is in front of them, and they paste it in. Sometimes that's right. Sometimes the tool is poorly suited to the task, the answer can't be checked, or doing the task yourself was the point.

### Four questions

1. **Is this a task AI does well?** Current models are strong at drafting, summarizing, restructuring, explaining, translating, and generating options. They are weaker when a task needs facts they can't look up, exact figures, or knowledge of your particular situation.
2. **What would a wrong answer cost?** A weak list of ideas for a club event costs nothing. A wrong dose, a wrong legal deadline, or a wrong number in a report someone will act on costs a great deal.
3. **Can I check the result?** If you know the subject, or can test the output, you'll catch errors. If you can't tell a good answer from a bad one, you're trusting the tool blindly.
4. **Would anyone else's data be involved?** A classmate's draft, a customer's record, and a friend's private message aren't yours to share with a vendor.

If the answer to question 3 is "no" and the cost in question 2 is high, think hard before delegating. That combination, a costly mistake that you couldn't detect, is where AI use goes most wrong.

### Trying the questions

| Task | Good at it? | Cost if wrong | Can I check? | Others' data? | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brainstorm names for a study group | Yes | None | Yes, by taste | No | Delegate freely |
| Explain a concept from today's lecture | Yes | I learn it wrong | Partly, against the textbook | No | Delegate, then confirm against the course material |
| Summarize a classmate's paper for peer review | Yes | Unfair feedback | Yes, by reading it | **Yes** | Read it yourself. It's their work, and reviewing it is your job. |
| Work out the right dose of a medication | Unreliable on exact figures | Severe | Not without expertise | No | Don't rely on it. Ask a pharmacist. |
| Write the reflection essay for this course | It can produce one | You learn nothing, and it's dishonest | Yes | No | Don't delegate. The reflection is the assignment. |

The last row shows something the four questions alone can miss. A further question sits behind them: **what is this task for?** If its purpose is for you to learn, practice, or give your own judgment, a tool that does it for you has removed the purpose.

### Delegation comes in degrees

The choice is wider than "AI does it" or "I do it". For most tasks you decide how to split the work:

| Arrangement | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **You do it; AI reviews** | You write the essay and ask for the three weakest points |
| **You and AI work together** | You talk through an idea, AI asks questions, and you write as you go |
| **AI drafts; you revise** | AI turns your notes into a first version, which you rework |
| **AI does it; you check** | AI reformats a reference list and you spot-check the entries |
| **AI does it alone** | A scheduled digest arrives each morning without your involvement |

Moving down the table, you hand over more of the work and take on more of the checking. The second half of this course is about the bottom two rows, where an agent does substantial work and your job becomes supervising it.

A good default for anything you'll put your name on is to stay near the top of the table until you know how the tool performs on that kind of task.

## Why a framework

Tools change every few months. By the time you graduate, the products in this course will have new names, new features, and competitors that don't exist yet. A framework gives you something that carries over. Faced with a tool you've never seen, you can still ask the four questions: what should I hand to it, how do I tell it what I want, how will I know the result is good, and what am I responsible for?

## Key terms

**4D framework**: Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence. **Delegation**: deciding what to hand to AI, and how much. **Description**: communicating the task clearly. **Discernment**: judging what comes back. **Diligence**: taking responsibility for the result. **AI fluency**: the ability to work with AI effectively, efficiently, ethically, and safely.

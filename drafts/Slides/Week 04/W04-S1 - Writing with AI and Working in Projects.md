## AI as a writing partner

- **Drafting**: get past the blank page
- **Editing**: tighten, restructure, change tone
- **Thought partner**: test your argument, find counterpoints
- **Summarizing**: long documents into what matters to you

The thinking is still yours.

---

## Good uses, risky uses

| Good | Risky |
|---|---|
| "Find the weakest step in my argument" | "Write my argument" |
| "Shorten this to 200 words" | "Add some citations" |
| "What would a skeptic ask?" | "Summarize this paper I haven't read" |

## Projects

A **project** is a persistent workspace:

- **Instructions** that apply to every chat in it
- **Reference files** (a knowledge base)
- **Project memory**: what it learns across chats
- Many chats about one body of work

---

## When a project beats a one-off chat

- Work that runs across **many sessions**
- Same sources, same instructions, every time
- A course, a research paper, a job search, a client

One-off question? Just chat.

---

1. Create the project

## Guided practice

Build a project for **your research topic**:

- Instructions: your question, audience, citation style
- Upload your 5–10 sources
- Ask it to map what the sources agree and disagree on
- Check one claim against the source yourself

---

## Responsible use

- **Authorship**: whose ideas and words are these?
- **Disclosure**: say how AI was used
- **Plagiarism**: AI text can echo sources closely
- **Copyright**: what you upload, and what you publish

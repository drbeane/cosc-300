# Workforce effects and AI-use policy
### Week 14
COSC-300/500 Applied AI Essentials

---

## Workforce effects

- Tasks change before jobs do
- Entry-level work is most exposed to automation, and most needed for learning
- New work: specifying, supervising, evaluating AI output
- The skills from this course

---

## A job is a bundle of tasks

| Moving toward AI | Staying with people |
|---|---|
| First drafts | Deciding what to write, and for whom |
| Summarizing | Judging whether the summary is right |
| First-pass analysis | Choosing the question, owning the conclusion |
| Routine code | Specifying, reviewing, owning what ships |

The right-hand column is this course.

## For your first years of work

- **Learn the underlying work**, even where a tool could do it
- **Build expertise in a field**: discernment depends on it
- Take the tasks AI is weak at
- Keep learning the tools

---

## What a good AI-use policy contains

- **Scope**: who and what it covers
- **Approved tools** and account types
- **Data rules**: what may and may not go in
- **Disclosure**: when to say AI was used
- **Accountability**: who signs off
- **Review**: when the policy gets updated

---

## What usable wording looks like

> Client financial records may be used only with tools marked "client data approved". Social Security numbers and bank account numbers may not be entered into any AI tool.

> The staff member who signs a piece of work is responsible for its accuracy, however it was produced.

Says what you **may** do. Names data by level. Names a person.

---

## Common policy mistakes

- Banning everything (people use it anyway)
- Naming specific products (out of date in months)
- No data rules
- No owner
- Vague wording ("use AI responsibly")
- Rules nobody can follow

A ban moves use onto personal accounts, out of sight.

---

## Test your draft

Read it as:

- a **new employee** on day one
- someone **in a hurry** with a client file open
- someone **looking for a loophole**

Then try it on two real cases from your capstone organization.

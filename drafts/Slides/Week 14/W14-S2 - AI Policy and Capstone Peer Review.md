## Workforce effects

- Tasks change before jobs do
- Entry-level work is most exposed to automation, and most needed for learning
- New work: specifying, supervising, evaluating AI output
- The skills from this course

---

## What a good AI-use policy contains

- **Scope**: who and what it covers
- **Approved tools** and account types
- **Data rules**: what may and may not go in
- **Disclosure**: when to say AI was used
- **Accountability**: who signs off
- **Review**: when the policy gets updated

---

## Common policy mistakes

- Banning everything (people use it anyway)
- Naming specific products (out of date in months)
- No data rules
- No owner

## Your capstone includes a policy

1-page **draft AI-use policy** for the organization in your capstone.

---

## Peer review: groups of three

- Read each draft against the **rubric**
- Written feedback for **two** classmates
- About 15 minutes per draft

---

## Rubric focus

- Is the **task and user** clear?
- Is the **tool and surface choice** justified?
- Does the **prototype** work?
- Is the **evaluation** convincing?
- Are **risks** named, with mitigations?

---

## Good feedback

- Specific: point to the section
- One strength, two improvements
- Suggest, don't rewrite
- Ask the question a skeptical manager would

# AI as a writing partner
### Week 4
COSC-300/500 Applied AI Essentials

---

## A new stage

Weeks 1–3: the model answers from what it learned in training.

Weeks 4–5: the model works from **your sources and files**.

The question for this stage: which parts of the work are yours?

---

## AI at each stage of writing

- **Planning**: find and test an angle
- **Drafting**: get past the blank page
- **Revising**: gaps, order, what a reader would miss
- **Editing**: tighten, fix, change tone
- **Summarizing**: long documents into what matters to you

The more of your own material it has, the more the draft is yours.

---

## The thinking is still yours

| Extends your thinking | Replaces it |
|---|---|
| "Find the weakest step in my argument" | "Write my argument" |
| "Shorten this to 200 words" | "Add some citations" |
| "What would a skeptic ask?" | "Summarize this paper I haven't read" |


---

## A test

When the piece is done:

> Could you explain and defend every claim in it without the tool open?

If you could, the thinking is yours.

---

## Getting honest feedback

"Is this good?" → "Yes!"

Instead:

- Ask for **problems**: "the three biggest weaknesses"
- Give it a **reader**: "a hiring manager with 40 applications"
- Give it the **rubric**
- "After each paragraph, tell me what you think my point is"
- "Don't rewrite. Tell me what to fix and why."

---

## Keeping your voice

"Improve this" → even, polite, formal, the same as everyone else's.

- Draft it yourself when it matters
- Limit the edit: "fix grammar, don't change my word choices"
- Show it a sample of your writing
- Ask it to list each change
- Read the final version aloud

---

## Responsible use

- **Authorship**: whose ideas and words are these?
- **Disclosure**: say how AI was used
- **Plagiarism**: AI text can echo sources closely
- **Copyright**: what you upload, and what you publish

---

## A useful disclosure

Not this:
> AI was used.

This:
> I used Claude to suggest a structure for section 2 and to edit the final draft for length. The argument and the sources are my own.

Rules differ by course and employer. Finding out is your job.

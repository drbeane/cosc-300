# Week 10: The capstone project

## What you will be able to do

- Describe what the capstone asks for and when each part is due.

Your capstone runs from now until the end of the term. You'll **design, prototype, and evaluate an AI-supported workflow** for a real or realistic task in your own field. It includes:

- your choice of tools and surfaces, with a justification;
- a working prototype: an agent workflow, a skill set, an app, or an always-on setup;
- an evaluation plan, and results;
- a risk and governance section.

## Choosing a task

The capstone works best when the task is one you know from the inside: something from your job, another course, a club, or a family business. You can only evaluate a workflow if you can tell a good result from a bad one, which is the Discernment requirement that has run through the whole course.

A good capstone task:

- **Recurs.** It's done weekly or monthly. A one-time job gives you nothing to evaluate.
- **Has a real user**, even if that's you. Someone does this task now and would notice an improvement.
- **Has checkable results.** You can say what a correct or good output is.
- **Fits the time.** You can build a prototype in about four weeks alongside your other work.
- **Is safe to prototype.** It doesn't need other people's private data or access to anything you can't afford to break.

| Too vague | Too big | About right |
|---|---|---|
| "Use AI to help with marketing." | "An agent that runs the store's whole social media presence." | "A weekly workflow that turns the store's new-arrivals spreadsheet into three draft posts in the owner's voice, for her to approve." |
| "AI for nursing students." | "A tutor for the entire nursing curriculum." | "A skill that turns one lecture's notes into NCLEX-style practice questions, checked against the textbook chapter." |

The projects in the right-hand column are small, and that is deliberate. A narrow workflow that you have built, measured, and assessed honestly is worth more than an ambitious one you can only describe.

Your prototype can take any form the course has covered: a project with instructions and sources (Week 4), an agent workflow with a skill (Weeks 6 to 8), a small app (Weeks 9 and 10), or, in the coming weeks, a program that uses the API or an always-on agent. Choose the simplest form that does the job. Explaining why you chose that tool over the others is part of the report.

## The proposal (due this week)

One page covering:

- **the problem**: what task, and why it matters;
- **the users**: who does this task now;
- **tools and surfaces** you plan to use;
- **what success looks like**, in terms you could measure.

### What success looks like

This is the part of a proposal that most often falls short, and it is the foundation of your evaluation in Week 12. State success so that someone else could check it.

| Vague | Measurable |
|---|---|
| "The posts will be good." | "The owner approves at least two of the three drafts each week with only minor edits, over four weeks." |
| "It will save time." | "Preparing the weekly report takes under 15 minutes, down from about an hour." |
| "The questions will be accurate." | "On 30 generated questions, a classmate who has taken the course finds no more than 2 with a wrong answer key." |

Each measurable version names what is counted, the target, and who judges. You don't have to hit the target for a good grade. You have to measure against it and report what you found.

### A short example

> **Problem.** Each week the owner of a small bookstore spends about an hour writing social media posts about new arrivals, and often skips it.
> **Users.** The owner, who writes the posts, and the store's followers.
> **Tools.** A Claude project holding past posts as style examples, with a skill that reads the weekly new-arrivals spreadsheet and drafts three posts. The owner reviews and posts them herself, so nothing is published automatically.
> **Success.** The owner approves at least two of three drafts with minor edits, each week for four weeks, and the task takes her under 15 minutes.

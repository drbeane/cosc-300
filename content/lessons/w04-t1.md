# Week 4: AI as a writing partner

**COSC-300/500 Applied AI Essentials** · 4D focus: Discernment

## What you will be able to do

- Use AI as a writing and thinking partner without handing over the thinking.

## AI as a writing partner

AI can help at every stage of writing:

- **Drafting**: getting past the blank page with a first version to react to.
- **Editing**: tightening, restructuring, adjusting tone, fixing flow.
- **Thinking**: testing an argument, finding counterpoints, asking what a skeptic would say.
- **Summarizing**: turning a long document into the parts that matter for your purpose.

The thinking is still yours. The most valuable uses push your thinking further; the riskiest replace it.

| Good uses | Risky uses |
|---|---|
| "Find the weakest step in my argument." | "Write my argument." |
| "Shorten this to 200 words without losing the main point." | "Add some citations." |
| "What would a skeptical reviewer ask?" | "Summarize this paper I haven't read." |

The risky column isn't forbidden; it's risky. "Add some citations" invites hallucinated references (week 3). Summarizing a paper you haven't read leaves you unable to check the summary.

## Responsible use

Writing with AI raises questions about **authorship** (whose ideas and words these are), **disclosure** (saying how AI was used), **plagiarism** (AI text can closely echo its sources), and **copyright** (what you upload and what you publish). The course policy asks you to disclose AI use; other courses and employers have their own rules, and you need to know them.

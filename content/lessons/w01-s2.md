## 1. Who makes the models

A handful of companies build most of the AI models you will meet:

- **Anthropic** makes Claude, the main tool in this course.
- **OpenAI** makes ChatGPT and the Codex coding agent.
- **Google** makes Gemini and NotebookLM.
- **Microsoft** makes Copilot, which runs on models from OpenAI and, in some configurations, Anthropic.
- **Meta** makes the Muse personal agent and the Llama family of models.
- **Open-weight models** are released for anyone to download and run on their own hardware.

This matters because the same task can go very differently on different vendors' tools, and because the tool your employer gives you may not be the one you'd choose.

## 2. Model tiers

Most vendors offer a family of models at different sizes. Claude's family is a useful example:

| Tier | Trade-off | Claude example |
|---|---|---|
| Large | Most capable; slowest and most expensive | Opus |
| Middle | A balance of capability, speed, and cost | Sonnet |
| Small | Fast and cheap | Haiku |

Bigger is not always better. For a quick rewrite or a simple classification, a small model is faster and uses less of your allowance. For a hard analytical problem or a long chain of reasoning, the larger model may get it right where the small one doesn't. Choosing a model is a judgment about the task.

## 3. Effort and "thinking"

Many current models can **reason** before answering: they work through the problem internally, sometimes at length, before writing the response. Tools expose this as an effort setting or a "think deeper" option.

More thinking helps most on problems with several steps where an early mistake ruins the result: math, logic, planning, careful analysis. It costs you time and usage. On simple tasks it's usually wasted.

In this course, model selection and effort levels are capability **C5**. You'll use the skill all term.

### What you can choose depends on the tool

| Tool | Models | Effort control |
|---|---|---|
| Claude Pro | Opus, Sonnet, Haiku | Yes |
| Copilot Chat (Maryville) | One model on the account we tested | Quick response, Think deeper, or Auto |
| Free tiers | Usually smaller models | Limited |

## 4. Lab: one task, five runs

Today's lab is the start of your first assignment.

1. Pick a realistic task from your own coursework or job. Good tasks have a clear purpose and something you can judge: summarize a reading for a specific audience, draft an email that has to strike a careful tone, explain a concept from your major, plan a week of study.
2. Run it on **three chat tools**: Claude, Copilot Chat, and one free tool (ChatGPT Free, Gemini, or another).
3. Run it on **two Claude models or effort levels** (for example, Sonnet and Opus, or the same model with and without extended thinking).
4. Save every prompt and every output.

As you go, compare the outputs on:

- **Accuracy**: is it right?
- **Usefulness**: would you actually use it?
- **Speed**
- **Format and tone**
- **What each tool could and couldn't do**: did it search the web, read your file, handle the length?

Start your comparison table during the lab.

## 5. Privacy settings

Before you use these tools for real work, check four settings in each:

- **Training on your chats.** Many consumer AI services may use your conversations to improve their models unless you opt out. Find the setting.
- **Memory.** Some tools remember facts about you across chats. Look at what's been saved and learn how to delete it.
- **Chat history.** How long conversations are kept, and how to delete them.
- **Consumer versus institutional accounts.** Your personal account and your Maryville account can have different terms. Know which one you're using.

### What never to paste

- Other people's private information.
- Student records, which are protected by FERPA.
- Confidential information from an employer or client.
- Passwords, API keys, or account numbers.

When in doubt, leave it out.

## Key terms

**Model tier**: a size class within a model family. **Reasoning model**: a model that works through a problem before answering. **Effort setting**: a control over how much a model reasons. **Model card / system card**: a vendor's published description of a model's capabilities, testing, and limits.
